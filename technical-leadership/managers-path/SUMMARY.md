# The Manager's Path - Subject Summary

A comprehensive recap of *The Manager's Path: A Guide for Tech Leaders Navigating
Growth and Change* by Camille Fournier, concept by concept.

**Progress note:** all 13 lessons are `drafted`; none have been discussed yet, so

See the progress table in [README.md](README.md). Reading order is top to bottom: the
individual-leadership foundations first (mentoring, tech lead, becoming a manager),
then the core people-management toolkit (1:1s, feedback, team health, hiring), then
scope that spans multiple teams and multiple managers (managing managers, technical
strategy, director), then executive scope (VP, CTO), and finally the meta-lesson on
navigating the transitions between all of these.

## Individual leadership foundations

- **[managers-path/01] Mentoring as the first leadership responsibility** - mentoring
  is management's low-stakes practice mode: help someone else be effective instead of
  being effective yourself, without any formal authority on the line.
  ([lesson](lessons/01-mentoring-as-first-leadership.md))
- **[managers-path/02] Tech lead responsibilities and hybrid leadership** - a tech lead
  stays hands-on while owning technical coherence and coordination for a team; delegate
  interesting work on purpose and lead through influence, not authority.
  ([lesson](lessons/02-tech-lead-responsibilities.md))
- **[managers-path/03] Becoming an engineering manager: role reset and priorities** -
  the job changes from personal output to team effectiveness; expect a real identity
  shift and a period that feels like loss before it feels like enough.
  ([lesson](lessons/03-becoming-an-engineering-manager.md))

## The core people-management toolkit

- **[managers-path/04] Running one-on-ones and building trust loops** - the 1:1 belongs
  to the report, not the manager; protect the cadence and follow through visibly to
  build the trust that surfaces real problems early.
  ([lesson](lessons/04-one-on-ones-and-trust.md))
- **[managers-path/05] Giving feedback and managing performance conversations** -
  feedback must be specific, behavioral, and timely; nothing in a formal review or a
  PIP should ever be a surprise to the person receiving it.
  ([lesson](lessons/05-feedback-and-performance.md))
- **[managers-path/06] Managing healthy teams: communication, conflict, and delivery** -
  diagnose whether a team problem is really communication, conflict, scoping, or
  process before reaching for a fix; build systems the team can run without you.
  ([lesson](lessons/06-managing-healthy-teams.md))
- **[managers-path/07] Hiring and interviewing as a management system** - a bad hire
  costs more than staying open longer; structure interviews around defined criteria and
  calibrate interviewers instead of trusting individual gut feel.
  ([lesson](lessons/07-hiring-and-interviewing.md))

## Scope beyond one team

- **[managers-path/08] Managing managers and creating leadership layers** - coach other
  managers' judgment instead of managing their reports directly; build deliberate
  visibility (skip-levels) since direct sight of team-level problems is now gone.
  ([lesson](lessons/08-managing-managers.md))
- **[managers-path/09] Technical strategy for engineering managers and directors** -
  managers stay technical enough to ask sharp questions and defend investment in debt
  and scalability, translating trade-offs into language stakeholders understand.
  ([lesson](lessons/09-technical-strategy-for-managers.md))
- **[managers-path/10] Director scope: organization design and cross-team planning** -
  team boundaries are an architecture decision (Conway's Law); build cross-team
  planning that surfaces dependencies early instead of as mid-quarter surprises.
  ([lesson](lessons/10-director-scope.md))

## Executive scope

- **[managers-path/11] VP of engineering scope: multi-org alignment and execution** -
  own execution predictability and cross-functional credibility across multiple
  directors' orgs; leverage comes from trustworthy systems, not personal visibility.
  ([lesson](lessons/11-vp-engineering-scope.md))
- **[managers-path/12] CTO scope: technology vision and company strategy** - the most
  variable title on the ladder; the common core is owning multi-year technology bets
  and company-level technical risk, distinct from a VP's execution focus.
  ([lesson](lessons/12-cto-scope.md))
- **[managers-path/13] Navigating growth transitions and your own leadership
  evolution** - every step up trades an expert skill for a beginner one; name the
  specific loss at each transition and treat continued climbing as a deliberate choice.
  ([lesson](lessons/13-navigating-growth-transitions.md))
