## Foundations: management as systems design

- **[elegant-puzzle/01] Engineering management as systems design** - treat the org as
  a system of components, interactions, and emergent behavior; trace symptoms to
  structural causes instead of patching them locally, and watch for queueing points and
  feedback loops. ([lesson](lessons/01-management-as-systems-design.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/02] Debugging organizations with systems thinking** - organizations
  are the way they are for a reason; assume rationality, trace decisions back with
  repeated "why," and treat a bottleneck that moves instead of disappearing as a clue.
  ([lesson](lessons/02-debugging-organizations.md))

## Team-level design and execution

- **[elegant-puzzle/03] Team sizing, composition, and cognitive load** - span of
  control and scope are two independent dials; "hire more" only fixes underutilized
  headcount, not incoherent scope or excessive cognitive load.
  ([lesson](lessons/03-team-sizing-and-load.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/04] Organizational design: functional, product, and matrix shapes**
  - each org shape optimizes for specialization, ownership speed, or flexible
  resourcing at a specific cost; Conway's Law means the shape shows up in your
  architecture whether you intend it to or not. ([lesson](lessons/04-organizational-design.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/05] Splitting and merging teams as the organization evolves** -
  split along real technical seams when span or scope outgrows a team; merge small,
  fragile teams with one clear leader; budget for the transition cost either way.
  ([lesson](lessons/05-splitting-and-merging-teams.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/06] Technical strategy as a management instrument** - a real
  strategy names a diagnosis and a policy that rules something out; a document
  everyone already agrees with isn't a strategy. ([lesson](lessons/06-technical-strategy.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/07] Planning and execution in medium and large organizations** -
  plans are forecasts, not commitments; the real value of planning is surfacing
  cross-team dependencies while they're still cheap to fix.
  ([lesson](lessons/07-planning-and-execution.md))

## Org-scale leadership systems

- **[elegant-puzzle/08] Hiring systems and onboarding for sustainable growth** -
  hiring and onboarding are pipelines with real bottlenecks and finite interviewer
  capacity; find the constrained stage before assuming the top of the funnel is the
  problem. ([lesson](lessons/08-hiring-and-onboarding-systems.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/09] Career ladders and calibration frameworks** - a ladder only
  works if two managers reading the same evidence reach the same conclusion;
  calibration is the recurring cross-manager mechanism that keeps it that way.
  ([lesson](lessons/09-career-ladders-and-calibration.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/10] Feedback systems and performance management** - feedback loses
  value with latency; name underperformance early with specific evidence and a real,
  time-boxed improvement plan, not a surprise at review time.
  ([lesson](lessons/10-feedback-and-performance-systems.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/11] Managing incidents and reliability as organizational practice**
  - clear incident roles, blameless postmortems, and a sustainably sized on-call
  rotation determine real-world reliability as much as architecture does.
  ([lesson](lessons/11-incident-and-reliability-practice.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/12] Reorganizations and change management without chaos** - a
  reorg needs a specific, statable structural problem, a tight timeline, honest
  communication of its trade-offs, and evaluation against that original problem.
  ([lesson](lessons/12-reorganizations-and-change.md))
- **[elegant-puzzle/13] Building resilient engineering leadership at scale** -
  leverage, not personal throughput, is what scales; delegate with real context and
  boundaries, and build a bench so the org survives the leader being unavailable.
  ([lesson](lessons/13-resilient-leadership-at-scale.md))
