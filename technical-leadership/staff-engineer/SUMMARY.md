## Role fundamentals

- [staff-engineer/01] What staff-plus means and how impact is evaluated - the job
  shifts from personal throughput to organizational leverage; impact is judged by how
  much better other people's decisions and work become, not by volume of output.
  ([lesson](lessons/01-what-staff-plus-means.md))
- [staff-engineer/02] Staff archetypes: tech lead, architect, solver, and right hand -
  four recurring shapes staff-plus roles take, distinguished by scope (team, domain,
  whatever's broken, an executive's priorities) and primary activity.
  ([lesson](lessons/02-staff-archetypes.md))
- [staff-engineer/03] Expanding scope from team outcomes to organizational outcomes -
  staff-plus impact lives in the seams between teams (duplication, interface
  friction, systemic risk, strategic gaps); claiming that scope is a deliberate,
  learnable practice. ([lesson](lessons/03-expanding-scope.md))
- [staff-engineer/04] Choosing an archetype that matches business and organizational
  needs - match personal strengths against an honest read of what the organization is
  currently starved for; reconcile mismatches by stretching, relocating, or hybridizing.
  ([lesson](lessons/04-choosing-archetype.md))

## Earning and communicating the role

- [staff-engineer/05] Earning the staff title: promotion packets, sponsors, and
  timing - promotion committees never see day-to-day work directly; do staff-shaped
  work first, build sponsor relationships, and make impact legible in a written packet.
  ([lesson](lessons/05-earning-staff-title.md))
- [staff-engineer/06] Writing strategy documents that align technical and business
  direction - real strategy is diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent actions; if a
  document contains no trade-off a reasonable person could disagree with, it's a wish
  list, not a strategy. ([lesson](lessons/06-writing-strategy-documents.md))
- [staff-engineer/07] Leading without authority through influence networks - influence
  is built from track record, relationships, and legible reasoning, earned
  continuously and spendable, unlike formal authority.
  ([lesson](lessons/07-leading-without-authority.md))
- [staff-engineer/08] Operating rhythms: planning, reviews, and executive
  communication - recurring forums (planning cycles, design review, executive
  updates) are the information infrastructure that keeps cross-team judgment current
  and visible. ([lesson](lessons/08-operating-rhythms.md))

## Execution, partnership, and long-term leverage

- [staff-engineer/09] Running critical initiatives across multiple teams -
  multi-team initiatives need an explicit plan, continuous unblocking rather than
  personal execution, and honest, frequent status reporting.
  ([lesson](lessons/09-running-critical-initiatives.md))
- [staff-engineer/10] Partnering effectively with engineering managers and peers -
  staff engineers and EMs cover complementary territory (technical direction vs.
  people/process); an unclear boundary is a common source of team dysfunction.
  ([lesson](lessons/10-partnering-with-managers.md))
- [staff-engineer/11] Multiplying impact by developing successors and technical
  leaders - the only form of impact that compounds after personal involvement ends;
  requires transferring real scope, not just giving advice.
  ([lesson](lessons/11-developing-successors.md))
- [staff-engineer/12] Sustaining a long-term staff-plus career and avoiding common
  traps - the recurring failure modes (permanent bottleneck, invisible busyness,
  always-on burnout, stale technical currency, archetype drift) and the periodic
  self-audit that catches them early. ([lesson](lessons/12-sustaining-staff-career.md))
