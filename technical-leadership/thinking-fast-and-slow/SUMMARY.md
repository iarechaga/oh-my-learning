## Core cognition models

- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/01] System 1 and System 2: two modes of thinking** - fast,
  automatic intuition (System 1) versus slow, effortful deliberation (System 2); nearly
  every bias in this subject is System 1 answering with unchecked confidence.
  ([lesson](lessons/01-system-1-and-system-2.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/02] Attention, effort, and cognitive load** - attention and
  System 2 capacity are finite, depletable resources; cognitive ease gets mistaken for
  truth, and load makes System 1 errors more likely.
  ([lesson](lessons/02-attention-effort-and-load.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/03] Heuristics as useful shortcuts and failure sources** -
  attribute substitution: hard questions get silently replaced with easier ones; the
  mechanism behind every named bias that follows.
  ([lesson](lessons/03-heuristics-shortcuts.md))

## Named biases: judgment under uncertainty

- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/04] Anchoring and adjustment bias** - any recently seen
  number pulls subsequent numeric estimates toward it, even when known to be
  irrelevant; sequencing independent estimates before any number is spoken is the fix.
  ([lesson](lessons/04-anchoring-and-adjustment.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/05] Availability bias and salience-driven judgment** -
  frequency judgments track ease of recall (vividness, recency, drama), not actual
  statistics, which distorts risk prioritization toward memorable incidents.
  ([lesson](lessons/05-availability-bias.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/06] Representativeness and base-rate neglect** - probability
  judgments track resemblance to a stereotype, crowding out actual base rates, even to
  the point of violating basic logic (the Linda problem).
  ([lesson](lessons/06-representativeness-base-rates.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/07] Overconfidence, planning fallacy, and optimism bias** -
  inside-view planning ignores the outside view's historical base rate, producing
  estimates that describe the best case as if it were the expected case.
  ([lesson](lessons/07-overconfidence-planning-fallacy.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/08] Confirmation bias and coherence illusions** - System 1
  builds the most coherent story from whatever's available (WYSIATI) and then searches
  for evidence that confirms it; the halo effect is a specific instance.
  ([lesson](lessons/08-confirmation-and-coherence.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/09] Framing effects and decision architecture** - logically
  identical facts produce different decisions depending on gain/loss wording; framing
  is unavoidable, so the skill is choosing it deliberately and honestly.
  ([lesson](lessons/09-framing-effects.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/10] Regression to the mean and causal misattribution** -
  extreme results are followed by more average ones purely from statistics, but System
  1 insists on inventing a causal story (an intervention "worked") to explain it.
  ([lesson](lessons/10-regression-and-causality.md))

## Judgment, risk, and choice under uncertainty

- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/11] Prospect theory: value functions and loss aversion** -
  gains and losses are judged relative to a reference point, and losses hurt roughly
  twice as much as equivalent gains feel good; drives the endowment effect and
  resistance to change. ([lesson](lessons/11-prospect-theory.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/12] Risk preferences in gains versus losses** - the fourfold
  pattern: risk-averse for high-probability gains and low-probability losses,
  risk-seeking for low-probability gains and high-probability losses.
  ([lesson](lessons/12-risk-preferences.md))
- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/13] The remembering self versus the experiencing self** -
  retrospective evaluation is dominated by the peak moment and the ending (peak-end
  rule) and nearly ignores duration; the self that decides is not the self that lived
  through it. ([lesson](lessons/13-remembering-vs-experiencing-self.md))

## Applied decision quality

- **[thinking-fast-and-slow/14] Practical debiasing for better decisions** - individual
  self-awareness rarely fixes bias in the moment; what works is structural: checklists,
  premortems, reference-class forecasting, structured rubrics, and shared team
  vocabulary for naming biases out loud.
  ([lesson](lessons/14-practical-debiasing.md))
