## Foundations

- **[staff-engineers-path/01] The three pillars of staff engineering** - the job is
  big-picture thinking x execution x leveling up others, not "senior engineer but
  more"; each pillar reinforces the other two, and neglecting one caps your impact.
  ([lesson](lessons/01-three-pillars.md))

## Big-picture thinking

- **[staff-engineers-path/02] Finding and framing high-leverage problems** - scan for
  pain that crosses team boundaries, frame it as cost x scope x "what solved looks
  like," and weigh it against your own scarce time before committing.
  ([lesson](lessons/02-framing-high-leverage-problems.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/03] Making better bets with strategic context** - every big
  technical decision is a bet; gather business/market/org context to make the bet
  informed, state its assumptions explicitly, and hedge under real uncertainty.
  ([lesson](lessons/03-making-better-bets.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/04] Seeing systems, not components** - trade some depth for
  a broad topographical map of the org's systems, ownership, and failure modes, so
  you can spot cross-system risk that a locally-deep expert would miss.
  ([lesson](lessons/04-seeing-systems.md))

## Execution mechanisms

- **[staff-engineers-path/05] Defining and communicating technical direction** - a
  written direction (current state, target state and why, principles, open
  questions) turns implicit tribal judgment into a shared, reusable reference;
  survives contact with real dissent before being called done.
  ([lesson](lessons/05-technical-direction.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/06] Navigating ambiguity with incremental execution plans**
  - big ambiguous mandates can't be planned end-to-end; narrow the ambiguity cheaply,
  commit to the smallest high-confidence next step, checkpoint, and replan.
  ([lesson](lessons/06-navigating-ambiguity.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/07] Driving execution through collaboration and
  delegation** - de-risk and keep the highest-ambiguity piece close, delegate the
  well-understood pieces widely (including across teams), track progress, and stay
  accountable for the outcome. ([lesson](lessons/07-driving-execution.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/08] Decision records and alignment artifacts that scale** -
  verbal alignment only covers the room it happened in; durable, lightweight
  artifacts (decision records, status updates) let alignment scale asynchronously
  and survive turnover. ([lesson](lessons/08-alignment-artifacts.md))

## Leveling up others

- **[staff-engineers-path/09] Raising quality bars with reviews and technical
  standards** - externalize your personal review judgment into checklists and
  worked examples so the bar holds in reviews you'll never personally attend.
  ([lesson](lessons/09-raising-quality-bars.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/10] Sponsoring projects so teams can move faster
  independently** - use your standing to open organizational doors (resourcing,
  visibility, credit) for someone else's work, then step back rather than taking
  over. ([lesson](lessons/10-sponsoring-projects.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/11] Mentoring and coaching for durable capability growth**
  - mentor when the gap is informational (tell them); coach when they already have
  the raw material and need practice exercising judgment (ask them).
  ([lesson](lessons/11-mentoring-and-coaching.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/12] Building communities of practice across teams** - a
  voluntary, cross-team group around a shared technical domain spreads knowledge
  peer-to-peer at a scale no 1:1 mentoring relationship can reach, if it has genuine
  grassroots pull and produces durable artifacts.
  ([lesson](lessons/12-communities-of-practice.md))

## Sustainability and organization-scale leverage

- **[staff-engineers-path/13] Managing your energy, reputation, and sustainable
  pace** - staff-level scope has no natural ceiling; actively budget your energy,
  reputation, and calendar as finite resources, and treat saying no as a required,
  recurring skill. ([lesson](lessons/13-sustainable-pace.md))
- **[staff-engineers-path/14] Becoming a force multiplier at organization scale** -
  the highest-leverage move at principal scope is often changing the system
  (tooling, process, incentives, people-systems) rather than solving a problem
  yourself, so future instances of it improve for everyone automatically.
  ([lesson](lessons/14-force-multiplier.md))
